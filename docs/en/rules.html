Rules

A short guide to Isle Routes for first-time players. When you are done, try it right away in Play the AI.

1. Goal

Four players gather resources and build roads, villages and towns. The first to reach 10 points during their own turn wins.

2. The island and resources

The island is made of 19 hexagonal lands. Each land produces one kind of resource.

LandProducesCount
🌲 ForestWood4
🧱 HillsBrick3
🐑 PastureWool4
🌾 FieldsGrain4
⛰️ MountainsOre3
DesertNothing (the robber starts here)1

Every land except the desert has a number from 2 to 12 (there is no 7). When the two dice add up to that number, villages and towns on the corners of that land receive its resource.

Takeaway6 and 8 roll the most often (5 times in 36), and 2 and 12 the least (once in 36). The dots under each number on the board show how many times in 36 rolls it comes up. You can roughly measure a village spot by adding up the dots around it.

3. Opening placement

  1. In seat order from 1st to 4th, each player places one village and one road touching it.
  2. Then, in reverse order from 4th to 1st, each player places a second village and road.
  3. Each player takes one resource from every land around their second village, and the game begins.

A village must be at least two edges away from every other village (never on a neighboring corner). This also applies during the game.

TakeawayYour two opening villages decide most of your income for the game. Compare the total dots, how many of the five resources you cover, and whether a harbor is nearby.

4. Your turn

  1. Roll the dice: every village next to a land with that number receives 1 resource, and every town 2 (this applies to all players).
  2. Build, trade and play development cards in any order, as often as you like (at most one development card per turn).
  3. End your turn: play passes to the next player.

Pieces are limited: 15 roads, 5 villages and 4 towns.

6. The 7 and the robber

  • On a 7, nobody receives resources.
  • Anyone holding 8 or more cards discards half of them (rounded down).
  • The player who rolled moves the robber to another land and takes one random card from a player with a village or town on it.
  • A land with the robber on it produces nothing when its number is rolled.
Takeaway7 is the most common roll (6 times in 36). If you end your turn holding 8 or more cards, you risk losing half of them.

7. Trading

On your turn you can trade 4 of the same resource for any 1 resource with the bank. With a village or town on a harbor corner, you trade at that harbor's rate.

  • 3:1 harbor: 3 of any one resource for 1.
  • 2:1 harbor: 2 of its specific resource for 1.

Trading between players is not available on this site yet (bank and harbors only).

8. Development cards

The deck has 25 cards. You cannot play a card on the turn you buy it (point cards count right away), and you may play at most one per turn.

CardCountEffect
Knight14Move the robber and take one card. Can be played before rolling
Point5Worth 1 point just by holding it (kept hidden)
Road building2Build two roads for free
Plenty2Take any two resources from the bank
Monopoly2Name a resource; every other player gives you all of theirs
TakeawayAt 9 points, buying a development card wins on the spot if it is a point card. In this site's research we found positions where the AI missed this and lost a lot of winning chances, and we fixed it.
