Isle Routes by the Numbers

Four copies of this site's AI (Normal) played 10,000 games. We measured the first-seat advantage, how winners get their points, and how the opening placement relates to winning.

1. Which seat has the edge?

Win rate by seat. The 1st seat won 27.3% (26.5%–28.2%), the most, and the 4th seat 23.5%.

TakeawayThe 1st seat gets the best first spot and the earliest turns. The 4th seat places two villages in a row, so make up for it with a good combination (all resource types, a matching harbor).

3. Longest road and largest army

Players holding the longest road at the end won 76.2% of the time, and those with the largest army 51.1%. Players with neither won only 3.3%.

TakeawayWithout the longest road or the largest army, winning is very hard (3.3%). Decide early whether you will take those 2 points by extending roads or by collecting knights.

Harbors

Win rate with an opening village on a 2:1 harbor: 27.3%; on a 3:1 harbor: 24.3%; no harbor: 24.9%.

5. How games unfold

  • A game lasted about 20 rounds (each of the four players took about 20 turns).
  • A player who was the sole leader in visible points at the halfway mark went on to win 43.0% of the time: better than one in four, but less than half hold on.
  • Winners bought 5.8 development cards on average, the others 4.7.
  • A 7 came up on 16.7% of rolls (the math says 1 in 6 = 16.7%).

Method: 10,000 games between four Normal AIs (one-move evaluation plus an exact search for wins available this turn). Boards, dice and the development deck are random. Ranges in parentheses are 95% intervals. These are AI-vs-AI numbers, so games between people may differ. The script is collect_stats.py.
